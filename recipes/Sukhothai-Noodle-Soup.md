## Notes

Based on Thai Spicy Noodle from Thai Thai Northampton. Traditional name is Kuay Teow Tom Yum or Sukhothai-style noodles. Ground pork is traditional but chicken is a common swap.

The key technique: season the bowl first before adding noodles and broth. It should taste sour first, then spicy, then salty with slight sweetness.

For richer "nam khon" style, stir 1-2 tbsp evaporated milk or a spoonful of Thai chili paste (nam prik pao) into each bowl.

## Instructions

1. Simmer chicken stock with daikon, cilantro stems, and garlic for 15-20 minutes. Season with fish sauce and sugar, keep at a low simmer.
2. Mix ground chicken with fish sauce, white pepper, and garlic. Drop small clumps into the simmering broth and cook 3-4 minutes until done.
3. Soak dried rice noodles in warm water for 20-30 minutes. Just before serving, blanch in boiling water for 15-30 seconds and drain. Blanch bean sprouts for 10 seconds.
4. Season each empty bowl with lime juice, fish sauce, sugar, chili flakes, and peanuts.
5. Add noodles and blanched vegetables to the bowl. Top with chicken, ladle hot broth over, and garnish with scallion, cilantro, extra peanuts, and fried garlic.
6. Taste and adjust -- add more lime, chili, or fish sauce as needed.
